# Week 12 Teacher Guide — Households and Teachers: The Obedience of Faith Completed

*The Obedience of Faith, Week 12 of 12 — the finale. Unit V (Commission). Companion to `Week12-HouseholdsAndTeachers.pptx` (7 slides, 25 click-stops). Scripture from the NET Bible.*

**Aim:** Land the course — God's order reaches from the assembly into the household, and maturity's destination is the Great Commission: "by this time you ought to be teachers."

**What this week must accomplish (and nothing more):** the class leaves with two things — a felt sense that the twelve-week arc is *complete* (the question that opened it has become the commission that closes it), and one concrete resolution (a name and a next step). This is the finale, so its job is different from every other week: not to teach a new doctrine but to *land* the whole course and *send* the class. The success test is not comprehension but *motion* — does each person leave with someone they'll teach and a first step they'll take this month? A course on the obedience of faith fails if it ends in agreement and no obedience.

**Tone: valedictory and warm.** After the two contested weeks, this week is a homecoming. It should feel like the end of a journey the class took together — grateful, hopeful, unhurried. Do not rush the ending; the willingness to let it land slowly, and to linger afterward, is part of the pastoral care of a final session.

**Where this sits in the arc:** everything converges here. Week 1 opened on "Why do you call me good?" and planted Hebrews 5:12 as a rebuke. Week 12 closes on "teaching them to obey" and turns that same Hebrews 5:12 into a commission. The four-layer stack from Week 2 returns, filled and perfect, in Christ. The deposit-and-handoff of Week 10 comes home to the kitchen table. The blemished-lamb of Week 9 is aimed at family time. The grace refrain — mercy first, altar second — gets its final repetition. Teach this week with the whole course in view; the callbacks *are* the content.

## Segment 1 — The Household: The First Congregation (0:00–12:00 · Slide 2)

Open by naming the journey: "Twelve weeks ago we began with a question — *why do you call me good?* Today we finish where Jesus finishes: *teaching others to obey.* The whole course has been one sentence, and today it lands." Warm, valedictory.

**Deuteronomy 6:6–7** (first click). Before the assembly, the home. The faith is handed down at the kitchen table, in ordinary time — "when you sit at home, when you walk along the road, when you lie down, and when you get up." Note the order: "on your heart *first*," then "teach them to your children." You cannot hand down what you do not hold — which ties straight back to Week 5 (you can only pass on what you've received and been trained in). Discipleship in the home is not a formal program; it's the overflow of a heart that holds the word into the ordinary moments of family life.

**Abraham and Joshua** (second click). This is *why* Abraham was chosen: "so that he may command his children and his household after him to keep the way of the LORD" (Gen 18:19) — election with a generational purpose. And Joshua's line at the end of his life: "as for me and my household, we will serve the LORD" (Josh 24:15). The household is a unit of covenant faithfulness that reaches across generations.

**Everyone carries a post** (third click) — **crucial for a mixed class; this is emphatically not a "dads" lecture.** Walk the three pairs: husbands and wives (love as Christ loved, Eph 5:25; and 1 Pet 3:7's startling wiring); fathers and mothers (raise them "in the instruction of the Lord," Eph 6:4; older women teach younger, Titus 2); grandparents and singles (Timothy's faith came through *Lois and Eunice* — a grandmother and a mother, 2 Tim 1:5). Read the Lois and Eunice example warmly and deliberately: the faith of the New Testament's most important young pastor came down through two women. Every single person in the room disciples *someone* — a child, a spouse, a grandchild, a younger believer, a friend.

**1 Peter 3:7** (fourth click). The striking connection: how you treat your family is *wired to your access to God.* Treat your spouse badly and "your prayers will be hindered." The household is not a break from worship or a lower-stakes zone; it is worship's *proving ground* — the place where the living sacrifice of Week 9 is offered *first*, before anyone sees it at church.

**Transition:** "And the pattern in the home is the same one we've traced all course."

## Segment 2 — The Pattern, One Last Time (12:00–24:00 · Slide 3)

**The one motion** (first click). Name the through-line of the entire course: you don't *generate* the faith — you *receive* it and *hand it on.* Deposit and handoff (Week 10), operating in the church and the home alike. The parent, exactly like the apostle, passes on *what was entrusted*, not what he invented. This unifies Unit IV (church order) with the household — same principle, two settings.

**2 Timothy 2:2** (second click). The relay verse, now seen to run through the *home* as well as the pulpit: Paul → Timothy → faithful people → others — and Timothy himself received it from Lois and Eunice. The chain runs through kitchens as much as through church buildings.

**The blemished lamb comes home** (third click). Week 9's Malachi image, now aimed at family time: if the time and attention you gave your household this week were an animal brought to Malachi's altar, what condition would it be in — the best of the flock, or the exhausted leftover? Deliver this *gently and first-person* — the people under our own roof are precisely the ones who most easily get our most tired, distracted, leftover minutes, while strangers and coworkers get our best. "I will not offer sacrifices that cost me nothing" (2 Sam 24:24) — and home is where the cost is realest.

**The ordinary shape** (fourth click). Dignify the small, unglamorous handoff: discipleship needs no platform or title. It is a life received in trust and handed to the next person — a child, a friend, a new believer, a grandchild. Everyone in the room is a link in a relay that reaches *back* to the apostles and *forward* past their own lifetime. That's a weighty and encouraging thing to be.

**Transition:** "And this is exactly where Jesus ends his time on earth."

## Segment 3 — The Commission: The Course Completed (24:00–36:00 · Slide 4)

This is the emotional and theological peak of the finale. Give it room.

**Matthew 28:18–20** (first click). Read it, hitting the bolded verbs — authority, go, disciples, baptizing, teaching — and **land hard on "obey"** (it's in red on the slide). This is the payoff the whole course has been building toward.

**The course's own title in the last command** (second click). Make it explicit: "teaching them to *obey* everything I have commanded you." Discipleship's goal, in Jesus' own final words, is not merely believers who *agree* or who *feel* — it is the *obedience of faith* (Rom 1:5), the very phrase that named this course. Twelve weeks on the obedience of faith, and here it is on the lips of the risen Christ as his parting charge.

**The Hebrews 5:12 resolution** (third click) — the full arc of the course, and the moment to let land. In Week 1, "though you should in fact be teachers by this time, you need someone to teach you the beginning elements" was a *rebuke* — a stinging bit of math about how little the readers had grown. Today the same verse is a *commission*: you have been fed the meat of the word for twelve weeks; *now you have the meal to serve.* The rebuke has become a calling. Say plainly: "Week 1, that verse stung. Today it sends you."

**The grace bookends** (fourth click). Notice how the command to teach is framed: it opens with "all authority is given to *me*" and closes with "I am *with* you always, to the end of the age." You are sent *under his authority* and *in his company* — never on your own strength or alone. The most daunting command in Scripture is bracketed by his power and his presence.

**Transition:** "And all of it — everything asked in twelve weeks — rests on something already finished."

## Segment 4 — The Stack, Completed in Christ (36:00–43:00 · Slide 5)

**Hebrews 10:5, 7, 10** (first click). Psalm 40 in Christ's mouth — "a body you prepared for me… I have come to do your will, O God… by his will we have been made holy through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all." The finale returns to exactly where Week 9 ended.

**The gold stack builds** (clicks 2–5) — bottom to top, one layer per click: Religion (perfect form), Worship (perfect heart), Sacrifice (the full cost, nothing kept back), Obedience of Faith ("I have come to do your will"). **This is a deliberate visual callback** — the same four layers the class stacked in Week 2, now shown *filled and perfect, in Christ.* If any class members also saw the sermon deck (*Trained to Discern the Difference*), this closes the loop visually. Point out the callback: "These are the four words from Week 2 — form, disposition, cost, settled pattern — and Christ held every one of them perfectly, once for all."

**The grace guardrail, one final time** (sixth click, the right column). He completed the stack *before* he asked you to climb it. Everything the course has asked — the tests of Unit III, the living sacrifice of Week 9, the ordered communion of Unit IV, the commission of today — *rests on his finished work*, never on your performance. Your sacrifice is a thank offering, never a payment. Say "mercy first, altar second" one last time, and note aloud that it has been the spine of the whole course — the guardrail at Weeks 4, 7, 9, and now 12.

**Transition:** to the last discussion, then the charge.

## Segments 5–6 — Discussion and the Charge (43:00–57:00 · Slides 6–7)

**The most warm-hearted discussion of the course — let it breathe.**

**Q1 — "Who taught the faith to you, and what did it cost them?"** Gratitude. Someone paid to hand you this faith — a parent, a grandmother, a teacher, a friend, a pastor. Naming them *and the cost* makes the relay concrete and moves people. Expect some emotion; that is exactly right for a final session. A few names spoken aloud is a fitting near-end to twelve weeks together.

**Q2 — "Whom will you teach, and what is the one concrete first step this month?"** Resolve, and it must be *concrete.* Push past "I'll be a better witness" or "I'll pray about it" to *one person* and *one step this month* — a specific conversation, a Bible opened with a particular child, an invitation extended, a text sent today. **This is the success condition of the entire course:** if everyone leaves with a name and a next step, the course landed; if it ends in warm agreement and no action, it didn't. Consider having people write the name and step down, or say them to the person next to them — commitment spoken or written is commitment more likely kept.

**The charge (slide 7, the final ~2 minutes — unhurried, valedictory).**

- **The standing assignment continues** (first click): the course ends but the habit doesn't. One chapter, before the phone, first minutes not last, eager and examining. The daily discipline was the point all along.
- **The whole course in one breath** (second click): read the arc slowly and let them feel the distance traveled — the Good is God's character (W1), sacrifice makes worship visible (W2), sincerity isn't enough (W3), righteousness is a gift (W4), discernment is trained (W5–6), every covenant tests faith (W7–9), authority is received and order serves communion (W10–11), and maturity's destination is to teach (W12). Twelve weeks, one thread.
- **From question to commission** (third click): the bookend — "You began being asked, 'Why do you call me good?' You end being told, 'Go… teaching them to obey.'"
- **The final banner** (fourth click): "Offer your sacrifices with the Obedience of Faith" — the course's title made imperative, its last word. Say it, allow a moment of silence, and close in prayer if that fits your setting. Thank the class for twelve weeks. Do not rush. Let it end well.

## Answers to Questions You May Get

**"I don't have children — or my family isn't believers. Does the 'household' part apply to me?"** Fully. The household principle is about *whoever God has placed in your life to disciple*, and Scripture is explicit that this extends well beyond biological children: Paul calls Timothy "my true child in the faith" (1 Tim 1:2) though they weren't related; the older women of Titus 2 teach younger women who may not be their daughters; every believer is commissioned in Matthew 28 to make disciples "of all nations." Single members often have *more* freedom to invest in spiritual children (Paul himself was single). And if your family aren't believers, *they* are your first mission field — 1 Peter 3:1–2 speaks directly to winning a spouse "without a word," by conduct. The relay needs every kind of link.

**"What if I feel completely unqualified to teach anyone? I'm still learning myself."** Two comforts. First, the Great Commission is given to *ordinary disciples*, not experts — the Eleven who received it had just been rebuked for doubt (Matt 28:17), and Jesus sent them anyway, with "I am with you always" attached precisely because they'd need it. Second, this is what Hebrews 5:12 addresses: you teach from *where you are*, handing on what you've genuinely received — a new believer can disciple a not-yet-believer; a one-year Christian can help a one-week Christian. You don't wait until you're a master; you hand on the meal you've been fed, and you keep eating. The relay runs one link at a time.

**"Isn't 'teaching them to obey' just legalism dressed up? Didn't Week 4 say we're saved by grace?"** This is the whole course's central resolution, so answer it with confidence. The command is "teaching them to *obey*" — but the obedience is the *response* to a salvation already given, never its *purchase* (Week 4, and the grace guardrail at Weeks 7, 9, and today). Notice the order in the Commission itself: *first* baptized (brought into grace), *then* taught to obey. And the whole thing rests on "all authority is given to *me*" and Hebrews 10's "once for all" — Christ finished the stack before he asked anyone to climb it. Obedience is faith's *shape*, not its price (the sentence from Week 4). A disciple obeys *because* he's saved, out of gratitude and love, not to get saved. Mercy first, altar second — one final time.

**"The course made a lot of demands. How do I actually keep this up after it ends?"** Honest and important — most courses evaporate within a month. Three practical anchors: (1) the *one habit* the whole course pointed at — one chapter a day, before the phone — because that single daily rep is what trains everything else (Week 5); (2) the *one relationship* from today's Q2 — a specific person you're discipling gives the whole thing traction, because we keep for others' sake what we'd drop for our own; and (3) *community* — don't do it alone; the household and the assembly exist precisely so we hand faith back and forth and hold each other up. Don't try to keep "all of it." Keep the daily chapter and the one person, and the rest follows.

**"What was the single most important thing in this whole course?"** A fair question to end on, and worth answering simply. If it's one sentence: *the Good is God's own character, and the obedience of faith is how a life becomes conformed to it* — received by grace, made visible in sacrifice, proven through testing, trained by daily discernment, and handed on to the next person. If it's one *practice*: the daily chapter, eager and examining. If it's one *guardrail*: mercy first, altar second. The course was never about information; it was about a life reorganized around what it claims to believe — which is the obedience of faith.

## Prep Checklist

- [ ] Read Matthew 28:16–20 and Hebrews 5:11–6:3 together — the Commission and the "ought to be teachers" resolution
- [ ] Reread your own Week 1 notes so the Hebrews 5:12 "rebuke → commission" arc is precise and moving
- [ ] Read 2 Timothy 1:5 and 3:14–15 — Lois, Eunice, and Timothy's home-grown faith
- [ ] Prepare to build the gold stack as a genuine callback — know the four Week-2 layers cold
- [ ] Decide how you'll invite concrete Q2 commitments (written down? spoken to a neighbor?) — motion is the goal
- [ ] Plan the ending: the banner, a silence, a closing prayer, thanks — and time to linger afterward
- [ ] Consider a one-page course recap or the "whole course in one breath" summary as a takeaway handout

## If You Run Long

The finale is the one week where you should *guard the ending above all else* — a rushed close undoes twelve weeks of work. Cut in this order: compress the Abraham/Joshua click on slide 2 (one sentence); trim the 2 Timothy 2:2 unpacking on slide 3 (the relay point is already made); shorten the grace-bookends click on slide 4 to a single line. **Never cut:** the "everyone has a post" material (it's what makes this a finale for the *whole* class, not just parents), the Hebrews 5:12 rebuke-to-commission resolution (the course's emotional peak), the gold-stack callback, Q2 (the concrete first step — the course's whole point is motion), or the final banner and the time to let it land. If discussion must shrink, keep Q2 and let Q1 be brief — gratitude can be felt in one or two names; resolve needs a real commitment. Better to end five minutes early with a proper landing than to rush the last word of the course.

## A Note on the Completed Course

With Week 12, all twelve weeks are built — deck plus teacher guide for each, across the five units (Foundations, Discernment, Testing, God's House, Commission). A few things worth doing now that the whole is in view:

- **A course-wide reading card.** The twelve weekly readings collected on one page (they're listed in the master outline) makes a clean handout for week 1 of the next run-through.
- **A recap/takeaway sheet.** The "whole course in one breath" paragraph from slide 7, plus the four recurring anchors (the Good is God's character · sacrifice makes worship visible · mercy first, altar second · read like a Berean), would serve as a fridge-magnet summary for participants.
- **The contested weeks (10–11)** carry the most facilitation risk; if you teach the course more than once, those are the two to debrief and refine after the first run.

If any of those would help, they're quick to produce. Otherwise — the course is complete.
