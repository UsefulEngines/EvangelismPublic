# The Last Eldership

### How a congregation loses its shepherds, and why it rarely notices until they are gone

Phil Pennington, with research assistance · November 2026 · Bible Research project

---

Every congregation that has lost its eldership can name the day it happened: the elders resigned, or the last one died, or no one stood. Almost none can name the day the eldership actually ended, because that came years earlier and nothing visible changed. The men still met. But what Paul described to the Ephesian elders, men whom "the Holy Spirit has made overseers" to guard the flock against wolves "from among your own group" (Acts 20:28–30), had already stopped existing.

The sequence has one root and four stages. The root is a question: where does the authority to teach come from? Either it is *conferred*, delivered by appointment and handed down as a deposit to be guarded (2 Tim 2:2), or it is *generated* by gifting, results, the will of the people, or the direction history seems to be moving. Paul answered in the passage that regulates the assembly: whoever "considers himself a prophet or spiritual person" must acknowledge that "what I write to you is the Lord's command" (1 Cor 14:37). No eldership ever votes to adopt generated authority. It is adopted the way a house settles, one reasonable accommodation at a time.

## Stage one: the elders stop teaching

The New Testament elder is first a teacher, "able to teach" and to "refute those who contradict" (1 Tim 3:2; Titus 1:9). The first accommodation is the quiet transfer of teaching to the hired man. The elders keep buildings, budgets, and personnel, and within a decade the congregation has a preacher and a board and calls the board an eldership. The loss is not felt until a teaching arrives that the men cannot answer, because refuting requires knowing and knowing was outsourced. A board can approve what the staff proposes. It cannot guard.

## Stage two: the vacuum fills

The space is filled by one of three things: a dominant man, John's Diotrephes who "loves to be first" (3 John 9); an outside authority, as when synods and finally the emperor settled what local elders could not; or the congregation deciding by majority, which is generated authority in its purest form, since it decides what it believes and decides again when the majority changes.

## Stage three: the deposit becomes a process

With no one whose office it is to keep the deposit fixed, it begins to change. This is never announced as a change in the status of Scripture. It arrives on one question, justified by one argument: that the New Testament is moving in a direction and faithfulness means continuing past the text. But that is a method, not a conclusion, and it does not stay on the question it was borrowed for. Bodies that used it to open the teaching office used it again, a generation later, to redefine marriage, having conceded that an instruction Paul grounded in creation (1 Tim 2:13) could be set aside by trajectory. An eldership that appoints women as elders uses the authority conferred by 1 Timothy 3 to set aside one of its own qualifications: the office amending the terms of the office.

## Stage four: the last eldership

This is the stage the congregation can date. Qualified men decline an office that has become criticism without authority; the congregation prefers a vote to overseers; the remaining elders cannot work together; the body has aged until it cannot field a qualified plurality. Most congregations in the movement now have no elders at all. None of these causes is heresy. That is the point. The last eldership is dissolved by the accumulated effects of having stopped, years earlier, being an eldership at all.

## What cuts the root

Hold conclusions because Scripture delivers them, not because they are traditional or popular. Have elders who teach. Refuse both the dominant man and the business meeting. Hold the narrow position on 1 Timothy 2 precisely and in full, restricting the one function Paul restricts and honoring every form of women's ministry the New Testament records, so nothing is left for a trajectory argument to grip. Keep the building, the pulpit, and the credentials local.

"The Holy Spirit has made you overseers." The authority was conferred, not generated, and cannot be amended by the men who hold it. An eldership that remembers this will not be the last one.
