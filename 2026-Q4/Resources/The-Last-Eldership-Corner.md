The Last Eldership

Every congregation that has lost its eldership can name the day it happened. Almost none can name the day the eldership actually ended; that came years earlier and nothing visible changed. What Paul described to the Ephesian elders, men whom "the Holy Spirit has made overseers" to guard the flock against wolves "from among your own group" (Acts 20:28–30), had already stopped existing.

The root is a question: where does the authority to teach come from? Either it is conferred, handed down as a deposit to be guarded (2 Timothy 2:2), or it is generated by gifting, results, or the will of the people. Paul answered: whoever "considers himself a prophet or spiritual person" must acknowledge that "what I write to you is the Lord's command" (1 Corinthians 14:37). No eldership ever votes to adopt generated authority. It is adopted one reasonable accommodation at a time.

The first accommodation is that the elders stop teaching hard scripture. The New Testament elder is first a teacher, "able to teach" and to "refute those who contradict" (1 Timothy 3:2; Titus 1:9). When teaching is handed to the hired man and the elders delegate to committees, the congregation soon has a preacher and a board and calls the board an eldership. A board can approve. It cannot guard.

The vacuum is filled by a dominant man (3 John 9), by an outside authority, or by majority vote, which is generated authority in its purest form. With no one whose office it is to keep the deposit fixed, the deposit begins to change. The change arrives on one question, justified by one argument; then group-think begins moving Scripture in a direction and faithfulness means continuing past the authoritative text. But that is a method, not a conclusion, and it does not stay on the question it was borrowed for. An eldership that uses this method to set aside one of its own qualifications is the office amending the terms of the office.

The last stage is the one the congregation can date. Qualified men decline an office that has become criticism without authority, the congregation prefers a vote to overseers, and the remaining elders cannot work together. None of this is heresy. That is the point. The last eldership is dissolved by having stopped, years earlier, being an eldership at all.

The remedy is the root: elders who teach, conclusions held because Scripture delivers them, and the memory that the authority was conferred, not generated, and cannot be amended by those who hold it. (http://TeachTheWord.Church)
